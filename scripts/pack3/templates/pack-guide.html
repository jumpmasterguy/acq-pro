Why defense finance confuses everyone

Several kinds of money, each with its own rules. A budget cycle that takes two years before a dollar reaches a contract. Cost accounting rules that would make a CPA reach for the aspirin. And enough acronyms to fill a DD Form nobody has seen since 1987.

Underneath, it follows a logic. Color of money exists because Congress wants to control how money is spent. EVM exists because the government needs to know a program is in trouble before it is finished. PPBE exists because you cannot build an aircraft carrier on a one-year plan. Each tool in this pack explains one of those logics on a page you can keep open.

Tool 1 · PDF

PPBE Cycle on One Page

The process DoD uses to decide what to buy and how to pay for it: Planning, Programming, Budgeting and Execution, with Congress in the middle. The sheet follows one budget, FY{{FY}}, across the whole timeline, and shows the four budgets that are always in motion at once.

The analogyPlanning a wedding two years out. You pick the venue long before you know the guest count, and by the day itself the money is committed. The only question left is whether you spend it well.
  1. Find the red Today line. Everything to its left is locked; everything to its right can still move.
  2. Read your program's year down the chart. If you need money in FY{{FY}}, it had to be in the POM built in {{FYm2}}.
  3. Check the execution bars. They tell you how long each kind of money can be put on contract.

Tool 2 · PDF

Color of Money Decision Tree

Which appropriation pays for a thing, decided by four questions asked in order. Page 2 has the periods of availability, the thresholds, and the RDT&E budget activity codes.

The analogyThree bank accounts: one for the mortgage, one for groceries, one for the kitchen remodel. You cannot pay the electric bill from the remodel account, even when it has money in it. In DoD, mixing them up is not a bookkeeping error. It can be an Antideficiency Act violation, and those are personal.
  1. Describe what you are paying for in one plain sentence.
  2. Start at question 1 and stop at the first yes.
  3. Check the thresholds on page 2, then confirm with your resource manager before you obligate.
Goes with Module 2, Lesson 4: Color of Money by Service

Tool 3 · PDF

EVM Formulas Quick Reference

Earned value management is how DoD tells whether a contract is on track for cost and schedule. Page 1 has the formulas and the four ways to estimate the final cost. Page 2 explains how to read them and walks through a worked example.

The analogyA road trip where you check the fuel gauge and the map at the same time. Fuel burned tells you one thing, miles covered tells you another. EVM compares both to where you planned to be, which is how you find out about the detour before you run out of gas.
  1. Get PV, EV and AC for the period from the IPMDAR or your program's EVM tool.
  2. Work out CPI and SPI, then the EAC range: the CPI method is usually the floor, the composite the ceiling.
  3. Compare TCPI with the CPI the team is actually getting. A big gap means the budget is a hope, not a plan.
Goes with Module 2, Lesson 2: Earned Value Management and Lesson 5: Estimate at Completion
Tool 4 · Excel

Wrap Rate Calculator

What a contractor charges the government for one hour of a person's time, and why it is well above that person's pay. It stays an Excel file because it is a working calculator: change the blue cells and everything else recalculates.

The analogyA restaurant steak. The $60 on the menu pays for the $15 steak, plus the cook, the rent, the manager, the accountant and a little profit. A wrap rate is that menu price divided by the cost of the steak.
  1. Enter a labor rate and the company's fringe, overhead, G&A and fee in the blue cells.
  2. Read the fully loaded rate and the wrap rate multiplier.
  3. Compare with the Rate Comparison tab, and match on business unit and work site, not just company size.
Goes with Module 2, Lesson 6: Wrap Rates & Cost Structure
