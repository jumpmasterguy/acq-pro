The default for running the force: most civilian pay, travel, training, supplies, services, maintenance and repair, and equipment at or under $350K per unit.

Three rules behind every answer

Purpose
Spend it only on what Congress appropriated it for.
31 U.S.C. §1301(a)
Time
The need must arise while the money is available (bona fide need). Severable services may run up to 12 months from award.
31 U.S.C. §1502; 10 U.S.C. §3133
Amount
Never obligate more than is available, or before it is available.
Antideficiency Act, 31 U.S.C. §§1341, 1517

Get it wrong and it is personal

A purpose or time error becomes an Antideficiency Act violation when the right money was not there to fix it. Violations are reported to the President and Congress, and the responsible person faces discipline. Knowing and willful violations carry up to a $5,000 fine and 2 years.

31 U.S.C. §§1349, 1350, 1351

Common traps

  • Splitting one system into sub-$350K buys to use O&M.
  • Using this year's O&M for next year's supplies.
  • Doing development work with O&M because it was on hand.
  • Calling an addition or conversion a "repair".

Special cases exist: working capital funds, lab revitalization, software pilots. When unsure, ask your resource manager before you obligate.

After the money expires

When its time to obligate runs out, money is expired. For 5 more years it can only adjust obligations made while it was current, such as in-scope cost growth on an existing contract. Then it cancels.

31 U.S.C. §§1552, 1553

Not the same $350K

The simplified acquisition threshold is also $350K (FAC 2025-06, Oct 1, 2025). It decides how you buy. The expense/investment threshold decides which money you use. Same number, different rules.
