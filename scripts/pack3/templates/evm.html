1The three numbers everything comes from

This program is over budget (AC above EV) and behind schedule (EV below PV). SV is measured in dollars, not days.

PV
Planned value. Budget for the work scheduled to be done by now.
Also BCWS, budgeted cost of work scheduled
EV
Earned value. Budget for the work actually done by now. The only number that measures progress.
Also BCWP, budgeted cost of work performed
AC
Actual cost. What that work actually cost.
Also ACWP, actual cost of work performed
BAC
Budget at completion. The whole planned budget: PV at the finish line.

2Variances and indices

MetricFormulaGoodBadSay it out loud
Cost variance (CV)EV − AC+ under budget− over budgetHow much the work done has cost more or less than its budget
Schedule variance (SV)EV − PV+ ahead− behindDollars' worth of work ahead of or behind plan
Cost performance index (CPI)EV ÷ AC> 1.00< 1.00Work you get for each dollar. 0.90 means 90 cents of work per dollar
Schedule performance index (SPI)EV ÷ PV> 1.00< 1.00Share of the planned work that got done
Variance at completion (VAC)BAC − EAC+ underrun− overrunHow far over or under budget it will finish
Percent variancesCV ÷ EV, SV ÷ PVUse these to compare work packages of different sizes

3Estimate at completion (EAC): four ways, one range

MethodFormulaAssumesUse it as
CPI methodBAC ÷ CPICost efficiency so far continuesUsually the floor. Overruns rarely finish below it
CompositeAC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI)Being behind schedule will also cost moneyUsually the ceiling
Budget rateAC + (BAC − EV)The rest of the work goes exactly to planOnly when the cause was truly one-time
Bottom-upAC + new ETCA fresh estimate of the remaining workTest it against the range above

Estimate to complete: ETC = EAC − AC. A contractor's own EAC is often called the LRE, latest revised estimate.

4To-complete performance index (TCPI)

TCPIBAC = (BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC)

Efficiency the remaining work needs to finish on budget.

TCPIEAC = (BAC − EV) ÷ (EAC − AC)

Efficiency needed to finish at the current EAC.

The reality check. Compare TCPIBAC with the CPI you have actually been getting. If TCPI is 0.05 to 0.10 or more above it, the plan is betting on a turnaround that almost never comes. Ask for a new EAC.

What CPI and SPI are telling you

IndexSignalDo this
0.95 and upNormal noiseKeep watching the trend
0.90 to 0.94DriftingFind the cause in the control accounts
Below 0.90Real troubleCorrective action; revisit the EAC

A common convention, not a rule. Your contract's variance thresholds decide what must be explained.

The 20 percent rule. Once a contract is about 20 percent complete, cumulative CPI rarely improves by more than 10 percent, and it usually gets worse. An early overrun is a forecast, not a blip.
SPI fades. As work finishes, EV catches up to PV, so SPI drifts back to 1.00 even on a late program. Late in a contract, read the schedule's critical path, not SPI.

Where the data comes from: the IPMDAR

The Integrated Program Management Data and Analysis Report replaced the CPR (Formats 1 to 5) and the IPMR. It arrives as data, in three parts:

CPDContract Performance Dataset: time-phased cost and hours
SPDSchedule Performance Dataset: the IMS
PNRPerformance Narrative Report: variance explanations

DID DI-MGMT-81861, current revision D (March 2026). The old "Format 1" WBS view can still be produced from the CPD.

FY2025 NDAA §823, applied by DFARS class deviation 2026-O0011, effective Feb 1, 2026. Firm-fixed-price contracts are normally exempt. Contracts with EVM get an Integrated Baseline Review soon after award.

A different tripwire: Nunn-McCurdy

CPI measures one contract. Nunn-McCurdy measures a major program's unit cost against its Acquisition Program Baseline. Significant breach: 15 percent over the current baseline or 30 percent over the original. Critical breach: 25 percent over current or 50 percent over original, which forces a formal review and, unless the program is certified, termination. 10 U.S.C. §§4371–4377

Words you will hear in the review

PMB Performance measurement baseline: the time-phased budget EV is measured against
CBB Contract budget base: negotiated cost plus authorized, unpriced work
MR Management reserve: budget held back for in-scope surprises, outside the PMB
UB Undistributed budget: in the PMB but not yet assigned to a control account
CA Control account: where scope, schedule and budget meet; owned by a CAM
WP Work package: the detailed task where EV is actually earned
IBR Integrated baseline review: joint check that the baseline is realistic
OTB / OTS Over target baseline or schedule: a formal re-plan when the PMB is no longer achievable

Try it: a worked example

A $10M contract. At month 12 the plan said $4.0M of work (PV). The team finished $3.6M of it (EV) and spent $4.2M (AC).

CV = 3.6 − 4.2 = −$0.6MSV = 3.6 − 4.0 = −$0.4MCPI = 3.6 ÷ 4.2 = 0.86SPI = 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = 0.90
EAC (CPI) = 10 ÷ 0.86 = $11.7MEAC (composite) = 4.2 + 6.4 ÷ (0.86 × 0.90) = $12.5MTCPI = 6.4 ÷ 5.8 = 1.10

Read it: expect $11.7M to $12.5M, not $10M. Finishing on budget would take 1.10 efficiency from a team running at 0.86, so plan for the overrun now.
