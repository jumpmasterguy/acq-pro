Free Acquisition Training Courses: What's Actually Free in 2026

"Free acquisition training" means different things depending on who you are. Here is the honest map.

If you are in the government acquisition workforce

  • WarU (formerly DAU). The whole catalog, no charge. This is the big one and nothing else comes close for DAWIA and CLPs.
  • DAU's Coursera licenses. Since 2018, DAU has bought Coursera seats for the workforce: agile software, Johns Hopkins data analytics, digital engineering. Free to you, paid for by the workforce development fund. Ask your training office; your organization submits your name.
  • FAI (fai.gov). The civilian-agency counterpart for FAC-C and FAC-P/PM. Free to federal employees.

If you are a contractor or trying to break in

  • WarU instructor-led courses, space-available. WarU's FAQ says there is no cost to attend and that "members of the defense industry (DoD contractors)" can enroll when seats are open. Request an account through SAAR, then expect to wait behind DoD employees. Possible, not dependable.
  • FAI's contractor training page and acquisition.gov. Public reference material and pointers to GSA Interact, the SBA Learning Platform, and NCMA. Useful, scattered, and not a curriculum.
  • Acqlerate's free tier. The full Foundations module (10 lessons) plus the first lesson of every other module, permanently free, no credit card. New accounts also get 14 days of everything. That is enough to know whether the platform is for you before paying anything.
  • The blog you are reading. Sixty-odd plain-English explainers, including what defense acquisition is, FAR vs. DFARS, and the complete guide. Free is free.

The pattern: the deep, structured, free stuff is reserved for the government workforce. Everyone else gets fragments for free and pays for structure. The question is only how much you pay. Management Concepts charges classroom prices; Acqlerate charges less than a sandwich.

DAU / WarU (Warfighting Acquisition University)

DAU, renamed the Warfighting Acquisition University in November 2025, is the gold standard for mandatory DoD acquisition training — and it's free for government employees. If you're a Contracting Specialist working toward DAWIA certification or need Continuous Learning Points (CLPs), DAU is where you go. The course catalog is enormous: hundreds of courses spanning contracting, program management, systems engineering, finance, and more. The content is authoritative and aligned directly with DoD policy and regulation.

That said, DAU has real limitations that anyone who's used it knows well. The experience is dense. Video lectures run from four hours to forty-plus. Navigation is clunky, and the platform doesn't translate well to mobile — not ideal for people trying to squeeze in learning between meetings. Access is built around the government workforce. Industry can get in on a space-available basis through the SAAR process (with DoD employees getting priority), and the general public cannot, which in practice cuts out most contractors and career changers. And because DAU is a government institution, course updates lag behind regulatory changes by months or even longer.

DAU is also primarily compliance-oriented: the goal is meeting certification requirements, not necessarily building practical intuition about how acquisitions actually work in the field. Plenty of people come out of DAU courses with completed CLPs but still fuzzy on the "why" behind the policies they just sat through.

Pros:

  • Free for government employees
  • DAWIA-aligned, CLP-eligible
  • Comprehensive catalog — covers every acquisition discipline
  • Authoritative and policy-accurate

Cons:

  • Workforce-first access — industry is space-available only, general public not admitted
  • Dense, slow-paced video courses (4–40+ hours each)
  • Poor mobile experience
  • Updates lag behind regulatory changes
  • No AI or adaptive learning features

Management Concepts

Management Concepts is the go-to choice for federal agencies that have training budgets and want structured, instructor-led professional development. Their courses are genuinely strong — particularly for Contracting Specialists and KOs, CORs, and program managers who need deep FAR/DFARS knowledge delivered in a classroom or live-virtual format. If your agency is paying and you want real interaction with an instructor who can answer your specific questions, Management Concepts delivers.

The price tag is the elephant in the room. Courses typically run $1,500 to $3,500 each, which puts them well out of reach for self-funded learners or contractors who aren't being reimbursed. There's no self-paced option — everything is scheduled instructor-led, which means you need to block calendar time and commit to a fixed schedule. Missed a session? You're often out of luck or paying to retake it. For someone trying to upskill around a busy program schedule, that rigidity is a real obstacle.

Management Concepts also doesn't offer any AI-assisted features, and the mobile experience is essentially nonexistent. Their strength is deep-dive classroom learning for professionals with an agency-funded seat — not flexible, on-demand learning for someone building skills independently.

Pros:

  • Excellent FAR/DFARS depth, especially for Contracting Specialists, KOs, and CORs
  • Instructor-led format allows real Q&A
  • Often qualifies for CLPs and certification credit
  • Reputable brand — well-regarded in government training circles

Cons:

  • $1,500–$3,500 per course — not self-funded friendly
  • No self-paced option
  • Fixed schedules require blocking dedicated time blocks
  • No mobile experience worth mentioning
  • No AI features

Coursera / edX Government Courses (and the DAU–Coursera Deal)

First, the thing people search for: DAU does have a Coursera partnership. It buys Coursera licenses for the government acquisition workforce, covering agile software development, data analytics, and digital engineering specializations from university partners, at no cost to the learner. If you are in the workforce, it is a good perk. If you are not, it does not apply to you, and it does not include acquisition content anyway. More on that in DAU vs. Coursera vs. Acqlerate.

Beyond that, Coursera and edX have government-adjacent courses — public policy, project management, even some procurement content from universities like MIT or Johns Hopkins. They're affordable, self-paced, and the platforms themselves work well on mobile. If you're completely new to government or public sector work and want a general grounding, they're a reasonable starting point.

But here's the problem: none of this content is DoD-specific. You won't learn what a DD Form 1423 is, how PPBE works, what the difference between a CPFF and an FPIF contract means in practice, or why source selection evaluation criteria actually matter. The courses are built for general audiences — MBA students, policy folks, international learners — and they read that way. Academic tone, theoretical frameworks, no FAR citations, no real-world DoD scenarios.

For someone who already works in defense acquisition and wants to get sharper, Coursera and edX won't move the needle. For someone trying to understand the DoD acquisition system specifically, they're a time investment that doesn't pay off in your actual job.

Pros:

  • Affordable (subscriptions or individual courses)
  • Self-paced and mobile-friendly
  • Certificates from recognized universities
  • Wide topic variety

Cons:

  • Zero DoD-specific content
  • Academic tone — not practitioner-focused
  • No FAR, DFARS, PPBE, DAWIA, or DoD acquisition framework coverage
  • AI features are generic platform tools, not acquisition-specific
  • Certificates carry little weight in the GovCon community

Frequently Asked Questions

Is DAU free for contractors?

Free, yes; available, sometimes. WarU (the renamed DAU) charges nothing, and its FAQ says defense industry members can attend instructor-led courses on a space-available basis after requesting an account through SAAR. DoD employees get priority, so expect waitlists, and the self-paced DAWIA curriculum is built for the government workforce. Contractors who need a reliable, structured path typically use commercial platforms like Acqlerate or Management Concepts.

What is WarU?

The Warfighting Acquisition University, which is the Defense Acquisition University under its November 2025 name. Same institution, same iCatalog, new domain (waru.edu). Old dau.edu links redirect.

Which acquisition training courses are completely free?

For the government workforce: all of WarU, DAU's Coursera licenses, and FAI. For everyone else: WarU seats when available, FAI's public resources, and Acqlerate's free tier (the Foundations module plus the first lesson of every other module, and a 14-day trial of everything). See the section above for the full breakdown.

Can I get DAWIA CLPs from Acqlerate?

Acqlerate does not currently issue official DAWIA Continuous Learning Points. For CLP-eligible training, government employees should use DAU or agency-approved providers. Acqlerate is designed to build practical knowledge and career skills, not to satisfy formal certification compliance requirements.

Is Management Concepts worth the price?

If your agency is paying, Management Concepts offers solid instructor-led training with real depth on FAR and DFARS. If you're self-funding, the $1,500–$3,500 per course price tag is hard to justify when platforms like Acqlerate cover DoD acquisition at a fraction of the cost. It depends heavily on whether you have reimbursement available.

How current is Acqlerate's content compared to DAU?

Acqlerate updates content as regulations and DoD policy change, aiming to keep pace with the acquisition landscape in real time. DAU content, as a government institution, typically lags behind regulatory updates by months — sometimes longer. For rapidly evolving areas like OTAs, cybersecurity requirements (CMMC), and PPBE reform, more current content matters.

Do I need prior acquisition experience to use Acqlerate?

No. Acqlerate offers novice, intermediate, and advanced tracks within each module. If you're brand new to defense acquisition, the Foundations module starts from scratch and builds up to practical applied knowledge. There's no assumed background — just a clear progression from basics to the harder stuff.
