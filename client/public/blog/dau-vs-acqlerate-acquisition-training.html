DAU vs. Coursera vs. Acqlerate: Which DoD Acquisition Training Is Right for You?

DAU (now WarU) is the government standard, Coursera is what DAU hands its workforce, and Acqlerate is the open alternative. Here's how to pick in 2026.

By Acqlerate · Updated September 2026 · 11 min read · For: USG & Contractor

TL;DR. Pick by your situation. DAU (now WarU) is free for the workforce and the only option here that delivers DAWIA certification training. Its Coursera access works like a staff lounge: badge required, and the menu is Coursera's general catalog, not WarU's acquisition courses.

  • In the acquisition workforce? Use DAU for DAWIA certification and CLPs.
  • Want tech skills too? Department of War employees get Coursera, LinkedIn Learning and more at no extra cost through WarU's Virtual Campus. It's general business and tech learning, not WarU's DoD acquisition curriculum.
  • Contractor or career changer? Contractors can open a WarU account and take some courses, but can't earn DAWIA certification or use the commercial learning perks. There's no general-public sign-up. Acqlerate needs only a name and email.
  • Want understanding, not just credit? Pair DAU for the credential with Acqlerate for plain-English lessons.

Updated September 2026. Two things changed since this comparison was first written. DAU was renamed the Warfighting Acquisition University (WarU) in November 2025, so the catalog now lives at waru.edu (the old dau.edu links redirect). And enough people search "DAU Coursera" that we added a section on what that partnership actually is, and who gets it. Short version: it is real, it is free, and it is for Department of War students (plus, for Coursera, the Federal Acquisition Institute's workforce), not for contractors or the public.

If you work in or around defense acquisition, you've heard of DAU, the Defense Acquisition University (now WarU). It's the official training arm of the DoD, and for government employees pursuing DAWIA certification, it's required. But for a growing population of contractors, career changers, and government employees who want more than just compliance credit, DAU's limitations are real.

Acqlerate is a commercial training platform built specifically for defense acquisition professionals. It runs on a different philosophy: practical understanding over checkbox completion, modern delivery over dense video lectures, and open access instead of government-only gatekeeping.

We make one of these products, so read this as an informed opinion. DAU is excellent for what it does. The question isn't which platform is "better," it's which one is right for you, based on your role, goals, and situation.

Access: Who Can Actually Use Each Platform?

This is the most important practical difference, and it's often glossed over.

WarU is built for the government workforce. If you're a DoD civilian or service member, access is no barrier: you open a WarU account through its SAAR form and take what you need. If you're a career changer from the private sector, a veteran who has already left service, or a student trying to learn about defense acquisition before your first job, there is no general-public sign-up. WarU's registration paths cover government personnel, industry employees and foreign nationals.

Contractors are the in-between case, and people argue about it, so here is what WarU's own help pages say. Industry students submit the same WarU SAAR form to get an account, which opens online training on the Virtual Campus, and a second request is needed to register for "some" classroom and virtual instructor-led courses (WarU help center). What contractors can't get is DAWIA certification: WarU says those certifications are only for DoW employees, and that it is "not aware of any source to certify contractors" (WarU certification FAQ).

So a contractor can get in the door, but only to some courses, and the curriculum is still built around the government workforce's certification needs.

Acqlerate requires a name and an email address. That's it. No CAC, no .mil email, no agency affiliation. A contractor in Norfolk, a veteran in Tucson, a business development manager at a small defense firm, a procurement professional trying to move from commercial contracting into defense: all can access the full platform within minutes.

If you're a contractor or career changer: WarU offers you partial access at best, and no certification. Acqlerate was built specifically for people in this situation: open access to serious DoD acquisition training, no government credentials required.

DAU on Coursera: What the Partnership Actually Gives You

This one confuses a lot of people, so here is the plain version. WarU (formerly DAU) gives its Department of War students free access to commercial learning platforms through its Virtual Campus: Coursera, LinkedIn Learning and Harvard ManageMentor (also open to the Federal Acquisition Institute's workforce), plus Skillsoft Percipio and Udemy (DoW only). WarU's homepage counts more than 60,000 learning assets across Coursera, LinkedIn Learning and Skillsoft Percipio alone.

What you get, if you are eligible:

  • Coursera: courses and certificates from universities and companies, on topics like data, software and management.
  • LinkedIn Learning, Skillsoft Percipio and the rest: shorter business and technology courses.
  • One login: you need a WarU ID and you enroll through WarU's Virtual Campus, at no additional cost.

Notice what these are: general business and technology libraries. They are not WarU's own acquisition courses, and they aren't built to teach DoD-specific topics like PPBE or DFARS clauses. You sign in through the Virtual Campus and work at your own pace.

Who is eligible: Department of War students, plus the Federal Acquisition Institute's workforce for Coursera. Not contractors, not career changers, not students. If you searched "DAU Coursera" hoping to get DAU-quality acquisition training through Coursera as a civilian, that door doesn't exist. Coursera's own public catalog has public policy and general project management courses, and they are fine for what they are, but they aren't built to teach you what a DD 1423 is or why the color of money matters.

If you are in the Department of War: use the free commercial learning, especially for data and tech skills. It is a good deal and it is already paid for. Then use Acqlerate for the acquisition side that Coursera doesn't cover. If you are not: Acqlerate is the platform built for you, and a month of it costs about a tenth of a month of Coursera Plus ($5.99 versus $59).

Cost: Free vs. $5.99/Month

DAU is free for government employees. This is a genuine advantage that isn't worth downplaying. It's a federally funded institution, and the training it provides is part of the government's investment in building a professional acquisition workforce. If you're a government employee and DAU is available to you, using it is the obvious financial choice for mandatory certification training.

Acqlerate costs $5.99 per month or $99 for lifetime access. To put that in context: multi-day acquisition courses from Management Concepts, a well-known commercial training provider, are listed at roughly $1,300 to $2,600 each. At $99 for lifetime access to a structured DoD acquisition curriculum with an AI study assistant, the price-to-value ratio is hard to argue with, especially for contractors and self-funded learners who aren't getting reimbursed.

Government employees using Acqlerate as a supplement to their DAU training should check whether their agency's training funds can cover a platform subscription. Some can, under professional development budgets.

Format: Long Video Courses vs. Self-Paced Micro-Lessons

WarU's formal training comes in three formats: online self-paced courses, classroom courses, and virtual instructor-led courses (its course listings sort them that way). Certification courses follow a set sequence. That structure makes sense for formal certification: you complete a defined body of material, demonstrate comprehension, and earn credit.

The practical problem, in our experience, is that most acquisition professionals don't have long blocks of time to dedicate to a single course. Formal courses often get started, paused, picked back up days later with the context lost, and finished more out of obligation than curiosity. That's not a criticism of DAU. It's the nature of how formal training works when delivered to a workforce that's simultaneously managing active programs.

Acqlerate is built around the reality of how busy professionals actually learn: in shorter sessions, around work, with the ability to skip material they already know and go deeper on the things they don't. The novice / intermediate / advanced track structure means you're not sitting through an explanation of what a contracting officer does if you've been one for five years. You jump to the level where you actually have knowledge gaps and fill them efficiently.

Real talk: There's a difference between sitting through a long course and actually retaining it. Both platforms deliver information. We built Acqlerate around short, structured lessons because we think that's how busy people actually learn.

Mobile Experience

In our experience, WarU's learning platform was not built with phones in mind. Navigating courses, watching video content, and accessing resources on a phone or tablet is a frustrating experience: not impossible, but clearly not a design priority. For learners who want to use commute time, downtime between meetings, or a lunch break for professional development, DAU's mobile experience is a meaningful barrier.

Acqlerate is mobile-first. Lessons, the AI study assistant, and module progress all work cleanly on mobile. This matters more than it might seem: the people most likely to benefit from acquisition training are often the busiest: active program managers, full-time contractors, government employees juggling multiple programs. If you can only learn on your phone between meetings, you need a platform built for that.

AI Features

WarU has started using AI too. Its website now has the WarU Assistant, an AI chatbot that answers questions about WarU courses, events, tools and general acquisition topics, and helps with account problems. It sticks to WarU's public information. What it isn't, as far as we can see, is a study tutor built into each lesson.

Acqlerate's AI study assistant sits inside the lessons and is set up for DoD acquisition questions. Ask it to explain the difference between a CPFF and an FFP contract in plain English and it gives you a real answer with practical examples.

Ask it to walk you through how PPBE works and why it matters for program funding, and it doesn't redirect you to a generic AI response. It answers in the context of the defense acquisition system. For people trying to build real working knowledge rather than just complete a course, this kind of on-demand, contextual Q&A is valuable.

DAWIA Certification and CLPs

This is where DAU wins outright, and it's not close. If you are a government employee required to maintain DAWIA certification in a specific acquisition career field (contracting, program management, engineering, financial management, or others), WarU is where the certification training lives, and DAWIA certification is only available to DoW employees. No commercial platform, including Acqlerate, replaces this function.

Acqlerate is not WarU training and can't give you DAWIA certification. Continuous learning is a separate requirement, at least 80 hours every two years for acquisition workforce members, and each Acqlerate module comes with a Certificate of Completion that you can self-report as external training. Whether it counts is up to your supervisor and your component's guidance. Use WarU for certification, and treat Acqlerate as a complementary resource for practical knowledge.

Content Depth and Currency

DAU's content is thorough and policy-accurate, built by subject matter experts aligned directly to DoD acquisition regulations and the DAWIA curriculum framework. In our view, where formal courses can lag is in catching up when the FAR, DFARS, or DoD acquisition policy changes: a government institution revises certification courses through institutional processes, and that takes time. To be fair, WarU does publish fast-moving guidance, such as its Revolutionary FAR Overhaul announcements.

Defense acquisition is not a static field. PPBE reform, CMMC implementation, OTA usage expansion, software acquisition pathway development: these are areas where the rules and policy are moving fast. Practitioners who rely on DAU training for current working knowledge may find themselves behind the curve on recent changes.

Acqlerate updates content as regulations change, with a nimbler update cycle than a government institution. For practitioners who need to stay current on the actual state of the acquisition system (not just what it looked like when the last course revision was approved), this matters.

Can I Use Both? (Yes, and Here's How)

For government employees, the best setup is often using both. They serve different functions:

  • Use DAU for DAWIA certification courses, CLPs, and official compliance requirements. It's free, it's authoritative, and it's required for certification. Check the boxes.
  • Use Acqlerate to actually understand the material. The practical intuition that makes you good at your job (understanding why certain contract structures make sense, how to read a program's financial posture, what's actually happening in a source selection) is what Acqlerate is built for.

They're complementary, not competing. A Contracting Specialist who completes their DAWIA contracting training through DAU and supplements it with Acqlerate modules will have both the certification credential and the genuine applied knowledge to back it up. That combination is rare and valuable.

Contractors and career changers who can't access DAU should treat Acqlerate as their primary training platform. It covers the foundational through advanced material needed to be effective in defense acquisition without requiring government credentials.

The Bottom Line

DAU is the right tool for what it's designed to do: mandatory DAWIA certification training for government acquisition professionals, delivered at no cost to the user. It's authoritative, comprehensive, and free. For government employees with certification requirements, there's no substitute.

Acqlerate is the right tool for everyone else, and for government employees who want more than compliance credit. Contractors, career changers, veterans transitioning into GovCon, small business BD professionals, and government employees who want practical applied knowledge rather than just completed CLPs will get more out of Acqlerate than DAU.

The question isn't really "DAU or Acqlerate." It's "which one serves my actual situation?" For most readers, the honest answer is Acqlerate, DAU, or both.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can contractors use DAU?

Partly. Industry students can get a WarU account through the SAAR form, take online training on the Virtual Campus, and submit a separate request to register for some classroom and virtual instructor-led courses (WarU help center). What they can't get is DAWIA certification, which WarU says is only available to DoW employees. There's no sign-up path for the general public. Commercial platforms like Acqlerate are the practical alternative for most contractors.

Is DAU the same thing as WarU?

Yes. The Defense Acquisition University was renamed the Warfighting Acquisition University in November 2025 as part of the department's acquisition overhaul. Same institution, same iCatalog, new name and a new domain (waru.edu). People still say DAU, and probably will for years.

Can I take DAU courses on Coursera?

Not exactly. WarU's own courses live in its iCatalog and Virtual Campus, not on Coursera. What WarU offers is free access to Coursera's catalog for Department of War students (and the Federal Acquisition Institute's workforce), through the Virtual Campus. If you are eligible, sign in with your WarU ID. If you are not, the partnership is not available to you.

Does Acqlerate count toward DAWIA requirements?

Not toward certification. DAWIA certification comes through WarU, and Acqlerate can't give it to you. For continuous learning, workforce members can self-report Acqlerate modules as external training using each module's Certificate of Completion; whether it counts is up to your supervisor and your component's guidance. Acqlerate is a knowledge and skill development platform: a complement to formal certification, not a substitute for it.

Is DAU content actually good, or is it just required?

In our opinion, it's good, particularly for regulatory and policy depth. DAU courses are built by subject matter experts who know the material. Our critique isn't about accuracy; it's about delivery and accessibility. Long courses on a platform that, in our experience, is clunky on a phone are hard for busy practitioners to get value from. The content is solid; the experience, to our taste, often isn't.

How long does it take to complete Acqlerate's Foundations module?

The Foundations module is structured with novice, intermediate, and advanced tracks, so completion time depends on your starting point and how deeply you engage with each lesson. Most learners can build a strong foundational understanding of the DoD acquisition system in a few hours of active engagement, without blocking out the long stretches a formal certification course asks for. The self-paced format means you go at your own speed.

I'm transitioning out of the military into a GovCon role. Which should I start with?

Start with Acqlerate. As a veteran, you likely have operational and program context, but the acquisition system (FAR, DFARS, contract types, source selection, PPBE) has its own framework that's worth understanding before your first GovCon job. Acqlerate's Foundations module will give you a working map of how the acquisition system operates from a civilian and contractor perspective. Once you're in a government role, you'll gain DAU access for any certification requirements.
