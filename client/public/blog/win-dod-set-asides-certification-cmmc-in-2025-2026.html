Win DoD Set-Asides: Certification & CMMC in 2025-2026

Navigate the evolving landscape of defense small business set-asides by mastering new certification mandates and the critical cybersecurity requirements shaping future opportunities.

By Acqlerate · April 2026 · 6 min read · For: Contractor

For small businesses in the defense sector, the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) isn't just a client; it's a strategic partner driving innovation and maintaining a competitive industrial base. As we look ahead to 2025 and 2026, the playing field for DoD small business set-asides is clearer but also more demanding. The emphasis is firmly on legitimate certifications, robust cybersecurity, and a keen understanding of where your capabilities fit into the DoD's strategic needs.

The federal government's commitment to small businesses remains steadfast, with statutory goals that include:

  • **Overall Small Business:** 23%
  • **Small Disadvantaged Business (SDB) / 8(a):** 5%
  • **Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB):** 5%
  • **Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB):** 5%
  • **HUBZone:** 3%

While the DoD consistently exceeds the overall small business goal, hitting those specific socio-economic targets, particularly for WOSB and SDVOSB, continues to be a focus. This means opportunities are there, but the path to securing them has become more rigorous.

SBA Certification: Your Non-Negotiable Entry Ticket

If you're an SDVOSB or WOSB firm eyeing federal contracts, including those with the DoD, the biggest change you need to internalize is the shift to mandatory certification by the Small Business Administration (SBA). Self-certification is a thing of the past, and contracting officers (COs) in 2025-2026 will be checking the SBA's official databases.

Program Old Process (Pre-2023) New Process (2023 Onward for 2025-2026 Opportunities) So What for Your Business?
**SDVOSB** Mainly self-certification, with VA VetCert (formerly CVE) for some contracts. **Mandatory SBA Certification.** As of January 1, 2023, the SBA is the sole certifying authority for federal SDVOSB set-asides. **Action:** If not already, start your SBA SDVOSB certification application immediately. Without it, you won't be eligible for set-aside awards. Plan for the administrative time involved.
**WOSB/EDWOSB** Self-certification or third-party certification (e.g., WBENC). **Mandatory SBA Certification.** As of December 31, 2022, SBA certification (or an approved third-party certification grandfathered in until that date) is required. **Action:** Ensure your WOSB/EDWOSB status is SBA-certified. This streamlines CO verification and reduces protest risk, making your firm a more attractive option.

So what does this mean for you? This mandate reduces the risk of fraud, strengthening the integrity of these programs. However, it also adds an administrative layer. For your firm, this means planning ahead. The application process takes time, so if you haven't started, you're already behind. Getting this certification is your fundamental gateway to set-aside opportunities in these categories.

CMMC 2.0: Cybersecurity is Now a Bid Requirement

Beyond eligibility, your firm's ability to protect sensitive unclassified information (Controlled Unclassified Information - CUI) is paramount. The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) 2.0 is the DoD's answer, and its final rule is widely anticipated for publication in early to mid-2025, with phased implementation stretching through 2025 and 2026.

CMMC 2.0 simplifies to three levels:

  • **Level 1 (Foundational):** Requires self-assessments.
  • **Level 2 (Advanced):** Requires third-party assessments (for most contracts involving CUI).
  • **Level 3 (Expert):** Requires government-led assessments.

Key Insight: CMMC 2.0 isn't just another compliance checkbox; it's a strategic differentiator. Firms that achieve the required CMMC level early will have a significant competitive advantage, potentially locking out unprepared competitors from lucrative DoD contracts.

So what does this mean for you? This is arguably the most significant hurdle for many small businesses. Achieving CMMC Level 2, which will likely be a prerequisite for a vast number of contracts involving CUI, demands substantial investment in time, resources, and potentially external consultants. If you haven't started your CMMC journey, you risk being excluded from future DoD opportunities. Begin by understanding the CMMC level your target contracts will likely require and budget for the necessary upgrades, assessments, and training now.

Decoding DoD's Goals & Finding Your Niche

Understanding where the DoD spends its money and where it struggles to meet goals can guide your targeting efforts. In Fiscal Year 2023, the DoD awarded approximately $77.8 billion to small businesses, exceeding its 23% goal. However, specific socio-economic categories often present unique opportunities:

  • **Small Disadvantaged Business (SDB) / 8(a):** The federal government generally meets or exceeds this 5% goal. The 8(a) program, with its sole-source capabilities, can see significant awards, sometimes exceeding $25 million for manufacturing or up to $100 million for services under specific conditions.
  • **WOSB:** The federal government achieved 4.63% against a 5% goal in FY23, indicating a persistent gap.
  • **SDVOSB:** The federal government achieved 4.54% against a 5% goal in FY23. This category remains particularly challenging for the DoD to consistently meet, despite the new SBA certification improving program integrity.

While set-asides vary widely, many contracts, especially for services, fall within the $500,000 to $5 million range, with plenty of opportunities both above and below. These figures highlight where the DoD needs to make up ground, creating targeted opportunities for eligible firms.

So what does this mean for you? If your firm falls into the WOSB or SDVOSB categories, the DoD is actively looking for you. This creates a strong incentive for contracting officers to set aside opportunities for these firms. Beyond that, research specific agencies and their small business utilization reports. Identify those struggling to meet specific goals and tailor your outreach accordingly. Focusing on niche capabilities that address unmet needs within these target agencies can further sharpen your competitive edge.

Your Action Plan for Success in 2025-2026

The evolving landscape demands a proactive and strategic approach. Here's your practical action plan to compete effectively for DoD small business set-asides:

  • **Prioritize SBA Certification (Now!):** If you're an SDVOSB or WOSB, your SBA certification is your golden ticket. Without it, you're out. Ensure your status is current and verifiable in the SBA's official databases.
  • **Invest in CMMC Compliance (Don't Wait!):** This isn't a future problem; it's a current competitive advantage. Understand the CMMC level your target contracts will require. Budget for assessments, technology upgrades, and staff training. Early movers will capture more opportunities.
  • **Strategic Teaming and Subcontracting:** Don't go it alone. Consider teaming with other small businesses to pursue larger contracts or leverage complementary capabilities. For 8(a) firms, this is crucial for growth beyond sole-source limits. For all small businesses, look for subcontracting opportunities with large primes, as they are mandated to meet small business subcontracting goals.
  • **Relentless Market Research and Relationship Building:** Actively engage with DoD agencies. Attend industry days, virtual events, and network with Program Managers (PMs) and Contracting Officers (COs). Use tools like SAM.gov, DSBS, and agency forecasts to identify upcoming solicitations. Understand their needs and position your firm as the expert solution provider.
  • **Focus on Niche Capabilities:** Small businesses often thrive by specializing. Identify specific unmet needs within the DoD – perhaps an emerging technology, a specialized service, or a particular geographic requirement. Position your firm as the go-to expert in that niche.

So what does this mean for you? This isn't a checklist to complete once; it's an ongoing strategy. The firms that will win in 2025-2026 are those that embed certification and cybersecurity into their core operations, actively seek out opportunities, and build strong relationships based on specialized value. Your proactive engagement on these fronts will directly translate into contract wins.

Conclusion

The DoD's commitment to small businesses remains unwavering, but the path to participation is becoming more rigorous and defined. For 2025-2026, the watchwords are **certification, cybersecurity, and strategic engagement**. Small businesses that proactively navigate the SBA certification process and embrace CMMC 2.0 will be best positioned for success. By understanding the rules, leveraging your socio-economic status, and preparing for the technological demands, your firm can effectively compete and secure vital DoD contracts, contributing to the nation's defense while growing your business.
