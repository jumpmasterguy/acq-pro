ACAT Levels: Your Program's Blueprint for Oversight and Management

Understanding your program's Acquisition Category (ACAT) is fundamental; it dictates the rules of engagement, from who makes key decisions to how your program is managed.

By Acqlerate · July 2026 · 7 min read · For: USG Personnel

The ACAT Foundation: Tiers of Oversight

If you're managing a defense acquisition program, you know that not all programs are created equal. Some are massive, multi-billion-dollar endeavors critical to national security, while others are smaller, more focused efforts. The DoD's Acquisition Category (ACAT) system is the foundational mechanism that recognizes this difference, providing a structured framework for oversight based on a program's estimated cost and criticality.

Think of your ACAT level as your program's "tier." This tier determines your Milestone Decision Authority (MDA) – the individual empowered to make critical decisions at program milestones, authorizing progression, committing resources, and establishing baselines for cost, schedule, and performance. In 2025-2026, while the Adaptive Acquisition Framework (AAF) provides flexibility, these core ACAT distinctions remain profoundly influential.

ACAT Level Cost Thresholds (FY2020 Constant Dollars) Typical MDA "So What" for Your Program
ACAT I (MDAPs) RDT&E > $520M OR Procurement > $3.17B USD(A&S) or delegated SAE Highest level of scrutiny, extensive documentation, formal milestone reviews. These are the "crown jewels" requiring top-level strategic alignment and oversight. Expect intense focus on cost growth, schedule adherence, and technical risks.
ACAT II RDT&E > $185M OR Procurement > $835M Service Acquisition Executive (SAE) Significant oversight at the Service level. While rigorous, there's often more tailoring potential than ACAT I. Focus remains on delivering capability efficiently within Service priorities.
ACAT III Below ACAT II Thresholds PEO or delegated to PM Most delegated oversight. Allows for greater agility and streamlined decision-making, often closer to the operational user. Emphasis on rapid problem-solving and meeting specific user needs.

So what? Your ACAT level defines your primary audience and the depth of detail they'll demand. An ACAT I program manager will spend considerable time preparing for OSD-level reviews, while an ACAT III PM focuses more on stakeholder engagement within their PEO or command. Misunderstanding your ACAT's implications can lead to wasted effort, delayed approvals, or worse, program failure.

ACATs in the Adaptive Acquisition Framework Era (2025-2026)

While the ACAT system provides the fundamental categorization, the DoD's Adaptive Acquisition Framework (AAF), fully entrenched by 2025-2026, has profoundly reshaped how programs within each ACAT are managed and overseen. The AAF's six tailored pathways—Urgent Capability Acquisition, Middle Tier of Acquisition (MTA), Major Capability Acquisition, Software Acquisition, Defense Business Systems, and Acquisition of Services—don't replace ACATs, but rather provide different lanes for them to operate within.

For example, an ACAT I program typically follows the Major Capability Acquisition pathway, adhering to traditional milestones and rigorous documentation. However, an ACAT I-level capability might leverage the MTA pathway for rapid prototyping, pushing for speed over traditional baseline stability. In such cases, the MDA might delegate decision authority lower, but still maintains strategic oversight to ensure the chosen pathway's principles are met. MDAs are now "pathway enablers," demanding clear justification for pathway selection and consistent execution.

Key Insight: The AAF allows for significant tailoring and delegation, but the underlying ACAT level still sets the initial expectation for MDA involvement. Choosing the right pathway is critical, but so is understanding your ACAT's inherent oversight demands.

Beyond the AAF pathways, other critical policies are shaping MDA oversight across all ACATs:

  • Mandatory Modular Open Systems Approach (MOSA): No longer optional, MDAs now scrutinize MOSA implementation plans at every milestone. For an ACAT I like the F-35 Joint Strike Fighter, retrofitting MOSA into an existing complex architecture for Block 4 upgrades is a massive undertaking under intense MDA scrutiny. Failure to demonstrate a robust MOSA strategy can lead to disapproval or conditions.
  • Digital Engineering (DE) as an Expectation: MDAs expect programs to leverage digital models and data analytics. This means less paper, more authoritative digital threads at reviews. PMs must present digital artifacts, demonstrating how DE reduces risk and optimizes design, testing, and sustainment.
  • Software Acquisition Pathway (SWP) Maturity: For software-intensive programs, regardless of their hardware ACAT, the SWP emphasizes rapid, iterative development. MDAs focus on metrics like software velocity, defect rates, and user feedback, rather than traditional hardware milestones. The Joint All-Domain Command and Control (JADC2) initiative's software components are prime examples, with MDAs focused on release cycles and demonstrated capabilities.
  • Supply Chain Resiliency and Security: Post-COVID and in a volatile geopolitical climate, MDAs demand detailed supply chain risk assessments, diversification strategies, and robust cybersecurity. Programs must identify foreign dependencies and single points of failure, a critical factor for any program approval.

So what? Your ACAT level still determines who your MDA is, but the AAF and other policies dictate what that MDA will focus on. You can't just follow the old rulebook; you must integrate these modern imperatives into your program's strategy, regardless of its ACAT.

Real-World Impact: ACATs in Action (2025-2026)

Let's look at how ACATs and their associated oversight play out in actual programs you might encounter:

  • LGM-35A Sentinel (formerly GBSD): A Classic ACAT I MDAP.

    This intercontinental ballistic missile program, with an estimated acquisition cost nearing $100 billion, is the epitome of an ACAT I MDAP. Its MDA is the USD(A&S), and oversight is rigorous and traditional. As Sentinel moves through its Engineering and Manufacturing Development (EMD) phase towards a Milestone C decision (expected 2027-2028), the MDA will be intensely monitoring cost growth, schedule adherence, and technical risks. There's little room for deviation from established baselines without significant justification. This demonstrates that for truly massive, strategic programs, the "Major Capability Acquisition" pathway and its stringent oversight remain paramount.

    So what? For programs like Sentinel, prepare for deep dives into every aspect of cost, schedule, and performance. Data-driven decision-making and meticulous adherence to baselines are non-negotiable. Your stakeholders extend far beyond the DoD, including Congress and the public.

  • Next Generation Air Dominance (NGAD): An ACAT I-Equivalent with AAF Flexibility.

    While highly classified, NGAD is conceptually an ACAT I-level capability. However, it's leveraging AAF principles like MTA for rapid prototyping and spiral development within the Major Capability Acquisition pathway. The MDA (likely delegated to the SAF/AQ or USD(A&S) for strategy) focuses on rapid iteration, competitive prototyping, and risk reduction. The goal is to avoid the cost overruns of previous fighter programs by emphasizing modularity and digital design.

    So what? Even for top-tier capabilities, the AAF allows for a different approach. PMs must be comfortable with accepting more risk in exchange for speed, demonstrating quick iteration and learning from prototypes. Your MDA reviews will focus less on fixed baselines and more on progress toward rapid capability delivery.

  • F-35 Technology Refresh 3 (TR-3) & Block 4 Delays: Sustaining an ACAT I MDAP.

    The F-35, a cornerstone ACAT I MDAP, is facing significant challenges with its TR-3 hardware and software upgrade, critical for Block 4 capabilities. With IOC pushed into 2025-2026 and lifecycle costs exceeding $1.6 trillion, the USD(A&S) MDA is demanding concrete plans to mitigate delays and control costs. This highlights that even mature ACAT I programs face intense scrutiny, especially regarding software integration, sustainment, and timely delivery of promised upgrades.

    So what? For sustainment and upgrade programs, your ACAT status means continued, rigorous oversight. Software integration, supply chain resilience for parts, and lifecycle cost management become paramount. MDAs will demand transparency and robust plans for overcoming unforeseen challenges.

Navigating Your ACAT: Key Takeaways for USG Personnel

Understanding your program's ACAT level isn't just an administrative detail; it's your strategic roadmap for success. Here's what you need to internalize:

  • Master the AAF, Don't Default: Don't automatically slot into Major Capability Acquisition. Explore MTA, Software Acquisition, or other pathways if they better suit your program's needs, but always be prepared to justify your choice to the MDA based on your ACAT's inherent oversight expectations.
  • MOSA and Digital Engineering are Table Stakes: These aren't just buzzwords; they're non-negotiable requirements for demonstrating technical maturity and future adaptability at any ACAT level. Your plans and execution for open architectures and a comprehensive digital thread will be scrutinized.
  • Software Demands a Different Mindset: For software-intensive components, shift from fixed milestones to continuous delivery and user feedback. MDAs will expect different metrics and review cycles, focusing on velocity, security, and demonstrated capability.
  • Proactive Supply Chain Management: Your MDA will demand transparency on foreign dependencies, single points of failure, and robust mitigation plans. Integrate supply chain resilience into your program planning from day one.
  • Data-Driven Decisions are King: Every justification to your MDA must be backed by robust, verifiable data. Leverage digital engineering tools to provide comprehensive insights into cost, schedule, performance, and risk.
  • Know Your Audience: Your ACAT level determines your primary MDA, but also the broader network of stakeholders (OSD staff, Congress, operational users) who influence decisions. Understand their priorities and anticipate their questions.

So what? Your ACAT level is the foundation, but the modern acquisition environment layers on specific expectations for speed, agility, and technical rigor. Success in 2025-2026 means not just knowing your ACAT, but also mastering how its oversight is shaped by the AAF and evolving DoD policies.
