Decoding DoD's Latest Spends: FY26 Contracts Point to Future Priorities

Recent large contract awards reveal the Pentagon's strategic shifts, emphasizing technological superiority and supply chain resilience against peer competitors.

By Acqlerate · June 2026 · 6 min read · For: USG & Contractor

TL;DR. Recent large DoD awards track the National Defense Strategy: AI, autonomy, hypersonics, cyber, space and a sturdier industrial base. If the strategy is the menu, contract awards are the receipt.

  • Contractor BD? Map your capabilities to those funded priorities and show rapid prototyping and DevSecOps.
  • Handling CUI? Since 10 Nov 2025, solicitations can require a CMMC Level 1 or Level 2 self-assessment. Third-party certification in solicitations (Phase 2) was suspended in July 2026 pending a review. If a solicitation calls for a self-assessment, no score in SPRS still means no bid.
  • Government PM? Tie requirements to strategy priorities, use faster pathways like Middle Tier, and write the right CMMC level in from day one.
  • Small or non-traditional firm? Primes are teaming with smaller innovators, and Other Transactions are the usual route for rapid prototypes.
As we near the end of the third quarter of Fiscal Year 2026 (FY26), the pattern of major Department of Defense (DoD) contract awards over the past nine months paints a clear picture of where the Pentagon is directing its resources. These aren't just isolated transactions; they are strategic investments reflecting the National Defense Strategy (NDS) and the urgent need to maintain a competitive edge against peer and near-peer adversaries. For both government program managers (PMs) and defense contractors, these trends show where the acquisition system is heading.

The Big Picture: Strategic Imperatives Driving Investment

Looking back at the awards announced since October 1, 2025, the overarching strategic imperatives laid out in the NDS have been consistently reinforced. The DoD isn't just talking about Great Power Competition; it's funding it. We've seen a clear prioritization of capabilities relevant to deterring aggression, particularly in the Indo-Pacific theater.

Technological superiority remains the bedrock of future defense. Awards have heavily favored next-generation technologies: artificial intelligence (AI), autonomy, hypersonics, advanced cyber capabilities, and resilient space systems. The push for Joint All-Domain Command and Control (JADC2), connecting sensors to shooters across every domain, is no longer a concept. It's an active, funded endeavor. Furthermore, a significant emphasis has been placed on strengthening the domestic defense industrial base and securing supply chains, a direct response to geopolitical vulnerabilities and critical material shortages.

So What? If your R&D, product development, or service offerings don't directly align with Great Power Competition, technological superiority, or supply chain resilience, you're likely out of step with the DoD's immediate priorities. PMs must ensure their program requirements are explicitly tied to these NDS pillars to secure sustained funding.

So What? This table isn't just a list; it's a roadmap. Contractors should scrutinize these areas to identify where their core competencies intersect with funded programs. For USG PMs, these programs represent the leading edge of modernization; borrow lessons learned from their rapid acquisition approaches.

Acquisition Evolution: New Rules of Engagement in Action

Beyond the specific programs, recent contract awards also highlight the practical impact of ongoing acquisition reforms and policy changes. These aren't theoretical anymore; they are embedded in solicitations and contract requirements.

  • Adaptive Acquisition Framework (AAF) Maturity: The AAF pathways, including the Software Acquisition Pathway and Middle Tier of Acquisition (MTA), are now routine. A March 2025 Secretary of Defense memo made the Software Acquisition Pathway the preferred route for software, and Secretary Hegseth's 7 Nov 2025 memo began turning the Defense Acquisition System into the Warfighting Acquisition System, moving programs under Portfolio Acquisition Executives. The CCA production award, which came about four months early, shows the speed DoD is after.
  • Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC): The CMMC program rule (32 CFR Part 170) took effect in December 2024, and the DFARS rule that puts CMMC into contracts took effect on 10 Nov 2025, starting Phase 1: Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments in solicitations. On 13 July 2026, DoD suspended Phase 2 (third-party C3PAO certification in solicitations, which had been set for 10 Nov 2026) and launched a 60-day review. Self-assessments, SPRS scores and DFARS 252.204-7012 still apply, and a contractor without the required self-assessment can still be ineligible.
  • Emphasis on Non-Traditional Contractors & Small Businesses: Newer companies are winning big pieces. Anduril took one of the two CCA Increment 1 production contracts, and Rocket Lab won $805 million of SDA's Tranche 3 Tracking Layer. Major primes are increasingly partnering with smaller, innovative firms to meet rapid prototyping and COTS solution demands.
  • Supply Chain Resilience & DevSecOps: Contract clauses related to supply chain risk management, domestic sourcing, and the implementation of DevSecOps practices are increasingly common. The DoD wants more transparency and resilience in how its critical systems are built and sustained.

So What? For contractors, CMMC is your "table stakes." At minimum, have your self-assessment score in SPRS, and if you handle CUI, keep preparing for Level 2 certification even while Phase 2 is paused. Furthermore, embracing agile DevSecOps and understanding the AAF pathways are no longer competitive advantages. They're baseline expectations. For PMs, actively using the AAF pathways and integrating CMMC requirements upfront are essential for efficient program execution and risk mitigation.

Your Playbook for Success: Actionable Insights

The patterns of FY26 awards offer clear guidance for both government and industry.

For Defense Contractors:

  • Strategic Alignment is Paramount: Continuously evaluate your R&D and business development against the NDS and the key funded programs discussed. Proactively pivot to areas like AI, hypersonics, and secure networking.
  • CMMC Compliance is Non-Negotiable: Get your required self-assessment done and posted in SPRS now. Third-party certification for Level 2 is paused, not cancelled, so keep preparing. It's a gatekeeper for future DoD work.
  • Embrace Agility & Innovation: The DoD wants rapid prototyping and iterative development, especially for software. Demonstrate strong DevSecOps capabilities and a willingness to partner with non-traditional innovators.
  • Shore Up Your Supply Chain: Be prepared to demonstrate supply chain resilience and transparency. Understand your foreign dependencies and proactively mitigate risks.
  • Focus on Affordability: Budgetary pressures remain. Solutions that offer high capability at a reasonable lifecycle cost will always be favored.
  • Invest in Talent: The demand for skilled engineers, software developers, and cybersecurity experts is fierce. Attract and retain top talent to stay competitive.

For Defense Program Managers (PMs):

  • Master the Adaptive Acquisition Framework: Don't just follow the rules; strategically apply the AAF pathways to accelerate capability delivery and manage program risk effectively.
  • Prioritize Cybersecurity: Integrate CMMC compliance into all contract planning and oversight from day one. Ensure your contractors and their supply chains meet the required levels.
  • Be an Industrial Base Steward: Actively monitor the health of your program's industrial base. Identify and address single points of failure, working closely with contractors to build resilience.
  • Use Data for Decisions: Use data analytics to inform requirements, track performance, and manage costs proactively.
  • Foster Innovation: Look beyond traditional primes. Actively seek partnerships with small businesses, DIU, and non-traditional defense companies to bring novel solutions to your programs.

The first three quarters of FY26 have solidified the Pentagon's direction: a relentless pursuit of advanced capabilities to deter peer competitors, delivered through more agile acquisition pathways, and underpinned by a strong, secure industrial base. Success in this environment hinges on adaptability, compliance, and a clear understanding of where the DoD is placing its bets.
