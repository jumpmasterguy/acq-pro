What Are PDUs — and Why Defense PMs Should Care

If you hold a PMP certification, you already know the deal: 60 PDUs every three years, or PMI revokes your credential. But if you're a defense program manager, contracting officer, or GovCon professional, there's a detail most people miss — the content you need to stay sharp in your actual job counts toward those 60 PDUs.

PDUs (Professional Development Units) are PMI's currency for continuing education. One PDU equals one hour of qualifying professional development activity. The key word is "qualifying" — and the bar is lower than most people think. PMI accepts self-paced online learning, and you self-report it to their Continuing Certification Requirements System (CCRS) with no approval process required.

The number that matters: 35 of your 60 PDUs must be Education PDUs — earned through structured learning activities like courses, webinars, or formal study. The remaining 25 can be Giving Back PDUs (volunteering, mentoring, creating content). This article focuses on the 35.

The PMI Talent Triangle: Three Categories, All Covered

PMI doesn't let you fill all 35 Education PDUs with a single category. They require a minimum of 8 PDUs in each of the three Talent Triangle areas:

  • Business Acumen — strategic thinking, benefits realization, financial acumen, regulatory/legal knowledge
  • Ways of Working — technical PM skills: data analysis, scheduling, risk management, methodology
  • Power Skills — leadership, communication, stakeholder management, negotiation

For defense professionals, this breakdown is almost tailor-made. The domain knowledge your career demands — acquisition lifecycle, budget cycles, earned value, contract types, capture strategy — maps directly to all three categories. You're not hunting for obscure PDU-eligible content. You're just formalizing the learning you should already be doing.

Why Self-Reporting Is Legitimate (and Common)

A lot of PMPs are skeptical about self-reporting. They assume they need a certificate from a PMI-Authorized Training Provider (ATP) or a formal attendance record. That's not the case for self-paced online learning.

PMI explicitly created the self-paced category to accommodate the reality of modern professional development — people learn from online courses, videos, articles, and digital resources outside of formal classroom settings. The responsibility is on the credential holder to report accurately. As long as the activity:

  • Advances your competence in a Talent Triangle area
  • Is a structured learning activity (not casual reading or browsing)
  • Took place during your current certification cycle

…it qualifies. Acqlerate's modules are structured, lesson-based courses with defined learning objectives. They meet the bar.

How to Log Your PDUs in PMI CCRS: Step by Step

PMI's Continuing Certification Requirements System (CCRS) is where you report PDUs. The process takes about 10 minutes once you know what to enter. Here's the exact flow:

  1. Go to pmlearning.pmi.org and sign in Use your PMI credentials. From the dashboard, click "Maintain My Certification" and select your PMP certification.
  2. Click "Add PDU" In your certification dashboard, find the "Report PDUs" or "Add PDU" button. This opens the PDU submission form.
  3. Select "Education" → "Online or Digital Media" Under Activity Type, choose "Education." Then choose "Online or Digital Media" as the sub-type. This is the correct category for self-paced online courses.
  4. Fill in the activity details See the card below for exactly what to enter for each Acqlerate module.
  5. Select the Talent Triangle category and enter PDU hours Choose Business Acumen, Ways of Working, or Power Skills (see the table above for which category applies to each module). Enter the PDU count to one decimal place (e.g., 1.9).
  6. Submit — no approval needed PMI accepts your submission immediately. You'll see your PDU balance update in the CCRS dashboard. Keep a personal log as a backup, but PMI rarely audits self-reported education PDUs when the activity is clearly legitimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Acqlerate an PMI-Authorized Training Provider (ATP)?

No — and it doesn't need to be for self-paced online learning. PMI ATP status is required for Instructor-Led Training that you want to claim as "Instructor-Led" PDUs. For online, self-paced courses, PMI's "Online or Digital Media" category allows self-reporting by the individual PMP. No provider pre-approval is required. Acqlerate qualifies under this category.

How does PMI define 1 PDU — is it really just 1 hour?

Yes. One PDU equals one contact hour of qualifying professional development. For self-paced online learning, this means actual time spent with the material — watching lessons, taking assessments, reviewing content. Acqlerate's PDU estimates are based on module completion time, measured conservatively.

What happens if PMI audits my self-reported PDUs?

PMI conducts random audits of PDU submissions. If audited for online/digital learning, they typically ask for documentation showing the activity occurred — a completion record, a receipt, a screenshot of your course progress, or similar. Keep a record of your Acqlerate course completions. Your account dashboard shows module progress and completion dates, which serves as your documentation.

Can I claim PDUs for Acqlerate content I completed before getting my PMP?

No. PDUs must be earned within your current 3-year certification cycle, which begins on the date you passed your PMP exam. Learning completed before your certification date doesn't count, and learning from a previous cycle can't carry over to a new one.

Does DAU training also count as PMI PDUs?

Yes. DAU (Defense Acquisition University) online training counts as PMI PDUs under the same "Online or Digital Media" self-reporting category. DAU courses have their own FAITAS tracking system for DoD certifications, but for PMI purposes, you log them in CCRS just like you would any other online course. The key is selecting the right Talent Triangle category when you report them.
